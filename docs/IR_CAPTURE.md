# IR capture procedure (Fisher and other spring tanks)

Owners: DSP Engineer (method), Dub Organiser (cataloguing). There are no hardware captures yet. This is the procedure for the first one.

## Equipment
- The tank under test, in its host unit (for example a Fisher K-10 or SR-series) or as a bare tank with a known driver and recovery amplifier. **Record which.**
- An audio interface at 96 kHz / 24-bit or better, with loopback for latency measurement.
- Cables to the unit's own input and output jacks (capture the whole unit's character), and, if possible, direct taps at the tank transducers too (capture the tank alone).

## Procedure
1. **Warm-up:** power the unit for 30 minutes (valves). Note room temperature, because spring tension drifts with it.
2. **Isolation:** put the tank on a foam pad, away from the monitors. Record 10 s of silence: that's the noise floor and the hum spectrum.
3. **Levels:** send a 1 kHz sine and find the input level for 1% THD at the output. Capture at **−12 dB** below that (the linear capture). Also capture at **0 dB** and **+6 dB** relative to the 1% point (the *driven* variants). Spring drivers are nonlinear, so these are different IRs, not just level changes.
4. **Signal:** exponential sine sweep (Farina), 20 Hz to 20 kHz, 15 s long, then 8 s of silence. Repeat 4 times.
5. **Deconvolution:** inverse filter, then average the 4 runs. Keep the linear IR and discard the harmonic IRs (keep them in `originals/` for analysis).
6. **Latency:** subtract the loopback latency so the IR starts at the true first arrival of the tank. Never trim away the tank's own arrival delay.
7. **Stereo:** for 2-spring tanks with separate pickups, capture each output as a separate channel of one file. Note *mono-in / dual-out*.
8. **Mechanical:** capture **no** IR for knocks, splash or drips. Those are nonlinear events, so the model handles them. Record them as separate samples for reference listening.
9. **Verification:**
   - Re-record a test rimshot through the hardware.
   - Render the same rimshot through Echolivity in IR mode with Drive 0 and that IR loaded.
   - Run `tools/compare_render.py`-style correlation on the two.
   - Then do a blind A/B with the Dub Producer.

   An IR can be marked `HARDWARE-VERIFIED` only after both steps, with the files saved as evidence.

## Naming
`<MAKER>_<MODEL>_<TANK>_<CONFIG>_<LEVEL>_<SR>`, for example `FISHER_K10_TANKA_UNITIO_LIN_96k`.
- **CONFIG:** `UNITIO` (unit input to output) or `TANKDIR` (transducers).
- **LEVEL:** `LIN`, `DRV0` or `DRV6`.

Synthetic IRs start with `SYN_`.

## Sidecar metadata (required; `tools/ir_catalogue.py` enforces it)
```json
{
  "id": "FISHER_K10_TANKA_UNITIO_LIN_96k",
  "authenticity": "HARDWARE-VERIFIED | HARDWARE-UNVERIFIED | THIRD-PARTY | SYNTHETIC - not a hardware capture",
  "hardware": {"maker": "Fisher", "model": "K-10", "serial": "...", "tank": "...", "service_history": "..."},
  "capture": {"date": "...", "engineer": "...", "interface": "...", "level_ref": "-12 dB re 1% THD",
              "temperature_c": 21, "method": "ESS 20-20k 15 s x4", "evidence": ["path/to/AB_test.wav", "notes.md"]},
  "sample_rate": 96000, "channels": 2,
  "processing_history": ["deconvolved", "averaged x4", "latency-trimmed 312 samples"],
  "license": "...", "attribution": "..."
}
```
The catalogue audit **fails** if any of these is true:
- An ID contains "FISHER" but isn't `HARDWARE-VERIFIED`.
- A `HARDWARE-VERIFIED` entry has no evidence.
- A WAV has no sidecar.
- The file's sample rate or channel count doesn't match its metadata.
- Two IRs have identical audio.

Raw recordings go in `ir/originals/<maker>/` and are never edited. Processed variants are regenerated by script into `ir/processed/`.
